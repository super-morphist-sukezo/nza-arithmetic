# N-Zero Ethics and Governance

## Residuals, Scarcity, and Non-Erasure in Social Protocols

**Abbreviation:** NZEG

**Status:** `proposal`

**Claim type:** normative design proposal

## Abstract

N-Zero Ethics and Governance explores a practical implication of the N-Zero
intuition: a person, complaint, obligation, or harm should not be treated as
nonexistent merely because it disappears from the currently visible account.

This is an ethical proposal, not a theorem derived from arithmetic or physics.
It must be evaluated through rights, consequences, power relations, privacy,
and real-world experiments.

## 1. Non-Erasure Principle

The central proposal is:

> A local administrative zero must not be treated as proof that the underlying
> person, claim, effect, or obligation has ceased to matter.

This is the social counterpart of the Local Zero Axiom. Removal from one account
or relationship may change the topology without erasing the node, transferred
effects, unresolved obligations, or future connection possibilities.

Examples include:

- closing a complaint without resolving its cause;
- disconnecting a node while leaving its dependencies unaddressed;
- recording a debt as paid while transferring the cost to another party;
- removing a message while preserving the power condition that produced it;
- reversing a decision while erasing evidence that the decision occurred.

## 2. Residual Is Not Automatic Truth

Preserving a complaint does not establish that every allegation is true. A
governance process must distinguish:

```text
Signal -> Protective action -> Investigation -> Decision -> Appeal
```

The signal remains attributable and reviewable, while access to sensitive
details may be restricted. Preservation must not become automatic punishment.

## 3. Abundance Horizon and Scarcity Discipline

An abundance interpretation of the universe does not remove local scarcity.
Time, care, housing, compute, attention, energy, and safe exit routes may be
severely limited for a particular node.

Therefore NZEG rejects the inference:

```text
global abundance -> local deprivation is unreal
```

Governance must measure access, timing, concentration, and substitution, not
only aggregate supply.

At the same time, a currently scarce topology must not be declared permanently
closed merely because its known paths are exhausted. NZEG therefore combines:

- **Abundance Horizon:** continue searching for new resources, transformations,
  habitats, and relationships beyond the current boundary;
- **Scarcity Discipline:** distribute currently reachable resources without
  borrowing against unverified future abundance.

Multiplanetary expansion may enlarge the resource and habitat mesh. It advances
this ethical goal only when launch, transport, communication, and settlement
rights do not become exclusive gateways controlled by a new central authority.

## 4. Rights Are Not Balancing Items

Basic safety, bodily autonomy, communication, emergency help, education,
healthcare, and protected exit must not be treated as collateral that can be
removed to make a ledger balance.

No conservation model can justify sacrificing one node because an aggregate
total remains unchanged. Equal totals can conceal radically unequal
distributions and coercive transfers.

## 5. Provenance and Privacy

Non-erasure creates a tension with privacy and the right to be forgotten.

A responsible ledger should separate:

- proof that an event occurred;
- the minimum data needed for accountability;
- sensitive content and identities;
- retention periods;
- access capabilities;
- correction and redaction records.

Cryptographic commitment, scoped access, aggregation, and deletion of raw data
may preserve accountability without preserving every detail forever. Technical
mechanisms do not replace consent or legal obligations.

## 6. Reversal Is Not Deletion

A Revert should restore a safer state while preserving the fact that the earlier
transition occurred. This supports learning and appeal.

At the same time, immutable public records can perpetuate harm. NZEG therefore
prefers layered records:

```text
public event receipt
restricted evidence
time-limited personal data
auditable correction or reversal
```

## 7. Relation to Aperture Mesh Protocol

NZEG and Aperture share a possible structural connection:

| N-Zero concern | Aperture mechanism |
|---|---|
| Local disappearance is not global resolution | Hold and unresolved state |
| A complaint must not vanish silently | Shared Monku and Issue |
| History matters after reversal | Commit and Revert provenance |
| A rejected path may remain viable | Fork |
| Disconnection leaves effects | Protected Exit and recovery obligations |
| One account must not define the whole | Inter-Mesh review and alternative paths |
| Current boundary must not become final reality | Boundary Revision and exploration |
| Expansion may create a new chokepoint | Replaceable gateways and protected exit |

This is an interpretive relationship, not proof that N-Zero validates Aperture
or that Aperture must adopt N-Zero ontology.

The continuity at issue is not forced permanent connection. A harmful edge may
need to end completely. What continues is the sovereignty of the node, the
accountability for transferred effects, and the possibility of other relations.

## 8. Governance Requirements

An N-Zero-informed process should:

1. name the boundary within which a case appears resolved;
2. record transferred costs and unresolved effects;
3. preserve appeal without preserving unnecessary personal data;
4. distinguish allegation, protection, judgment, and enforcement;
5. prevent aggregate conservation from overriding individual rights;
6. make reviewers and enforcement paths replaceable;
7. expose who can write, read, revise, and delete each record;
8. allow Exit without erasing obligations owed to the exiting node.
9. distinguish total possible resources from resources a node can actually
   reach and use.
10. prevent infrastructure gateways from converting exploration into enclosure.

## 9. Failure Modes

### Infinite-ledger surveillance

Keeping every residual forever creates permanent surveillance and prevents
rehabilitation.

### Metaphysical dismissal of scarcity

Calling resources infinite can obscure deprivation and justify withholding
practical support.

### Gateway capture

New territories or resources may increase nominal abundance while transport,
energy, communication, or permission remains controlled by a small group.
Expansion without independent paths can reproduce oligopoly at a larger scale.

### Automatic truth from persistence

A preserved allegation may be mistaken for a proven fact.

### Conservation without distribution

A system may preserve total wealth while concentrating it in one node.

### Unowned environment account

Costs assigned to an abstract environment may actually be borne by children,
workers, future participants, or ecosystems without representation.

## 10. Evaluation Questions

Pilots should ask:

- Did unresolved effects become more visible?
- Were false reports and contested interpretations handled without retaliation?
- Did record retention create new privacy or power risks?
- Could participants correct, appeal, and exit?
- Were transferred costs assigned to identifiable decision makers?
- Did the process improve outcomes compared with a simpler workflow?

## 11. Normative Claim

The strongest defensible ethical claim is:

> Do not mistake removal from the current interface for resolution of the
> underlying relationship. Preserve enough provenance to find transferred harm,
> while preserving enough privacy and reversibility for people to recover.
